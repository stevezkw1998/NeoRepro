# Response to independent reviewers

Date: 2026-08-20

## Response to Reviewer A

1. **Leakage dimensions and near sequence — addressed.** `scripts/audit_prime2_overlap.py` now emits exact-peptide, exact peptide–HLA and same-HLA/same-length Hamming-distance-one results. Mutation, patient and study identity are explicitly `unavailable` because PRIME2 Table S4 lacks those identifiers. The primary exclusion is exact peptide–HLA because peptide specificity is HLA-conditioned. Separate versioned sensitivities exclude the 35 retained peptides seen under another HLA and the 18 distance-one records; both leave BigMHC/PRIME AUROC essentially unchanged. The protocol and research log were updated.
2. **Clean reproduction — addressed before finalization.** The manifest was expanded to include the full benchmark, audit summaries, all fixed and baseline predictions, folds, sensitivity analyses, HLA outputs, tables, figures, manuscript, references, review records, model manifest and full-run receipt. A clean-checkout reproduction receipt is produced after committing the complete artifact set; the final report records its commit and outcome.
3. **Baseline fairness — addressed.** HLA-only, peptide-only and HLA-plus-peptide baselines now use the same class-weighted logistic-regression estimator, scaler, solver and fold structure. They differ only by feature set. Methods now correctly describe peptide length and amino-acid composition, not positional features.
4. **Top-K ties — addressed.** `patient_values` now calculates analytic expectations over tied score groups for Recall, Precision, HitRate, NDCG and reciprocal rank. A deterministic unit test covers a boundary tie. HLA-only Recall@20 moved to 0.121 (LOPO) and 0.119 (LOSO), consistent with the reviewer's random-tie estimates.
5. **Bootstrap scope — clarified.** We did not add a computationally expensive refit bootstrap. Baseline intervals are explicitly labeled in Methods and the Table 2 caption as conditional on the frozen out-of-fold predictions and not including fitting variation. LOSO is described as three observed compound domains, with no inference to a population of studies.
6. **HLA inference — downgraded.** The manuscript calls the variance fraction score-scale-specific and unadjusted, does not compare it across score transformations, and does not infer an isolated allele effect. HLA permutation outputs remain machine-readable exploratory diagnostics but support no biological significance claim.
7. **Length domain — addressed.** A 15,234-record 9–10mer sensitivity analysis gives AUROC 0.547 for BigMHC and 0.605 for PRIME, preserving the primary direction.
8. **Primary comparison — addressed transparently.** Pooled AUROC for PRIME versus BigMHC on common support is the final post-review reporting primary, explicitly not preregistered as primary. The dated specification and research log preserve the original Top-K emphasis and the reason for the reporting revision. AP, Top-K, HLA and cohort results are supporting or exploratory.
9. **Artifact evidence and dynamic manifest — addressed.** A 206,810,982-byte MHCflurry model tree is frozen by a 58-file SHA-256 manifest. The unified runner records commands, per-tool runtime, platform, return code and output hashes. Benchmark counts, predictor versions and evaluation configuration are derived from artifacts.
10. **Minor comments — addressed or bounded.** The manuscript reports a random Recall@20 reference, removes calibration language, notes context-dependent repeated records and uses a negation-aware overclaim audit.

## Response to Reviewer B

1. **Decision unit — addressed.** The primary analysis is explicitly patient-level pMHC-pair retrieval. A patient–peptide sensitivity aggregates labels by any-HLA positive and scores by the maximum oriented HLA score. It contains 15,508 candidates; Recall@20 becomes 0.166, 0.220 and 0.304 for BigMHC, MHCflurry and PRIME, without changing the qualitative ordering. Because raw scales vary across HLA, an added sensitivity first converts each tool's scores to within-HLA empirical mid-percentiles and then takes the maximum; Recall@20 becomes 0.202, 0.215 and 0.240. The manuscript labels both peptide aggregations exploratory; the ordering is preserved but the gap narrows.
2. **Endpoint terminology — addressed.** The manuscript now uses “DNA-barcoded pMHC multimer-detectable patient-matched T-cell recognition” and identifies `immunogenicity` only as an operational schema field. Natural processing, tumor presentation, function, killing and benefit are explicitly outside the evidence.
3. **MHCflurry task and invocation — addressed.** MHCflurry is excluded from the main immunogenicity table, labeled a peptide/HLA-only invocation without flanking context, and interpreted only as association with the recognition endpoint, not presentation validation.
4. **Candidate preselection — addressed.** The title, abstract Methods, dataset Methods, conclusion and limitations identify the MuPeXI/RNA/NetMHCpan-oriented gate and restrict inference to reranking those candidates.
5. **Patient immune context — addressed.** Cross-patient label conflicts and missing TCR repertoire, treatment and microenvironment inputs are explicit limitations; “patient-level” describes grouped evaluation, not individualized immune-state modeling.
6. **Cohort interpretation — addressed.** Cohorts are called compound domains. BigMHC's observed cohort AUROC range is reported, and the manuscript states that cancer, treatment, sample source, assay and HLA effects cannot be separated.
7. **HLA support — addressed.** Per-HLA output now includes n, positives, patients and studies and marks fewer than three positives or patients as unsupported for interpretation. The between-HLA variance wording was narrowed.
8. **Positive-bearing patients — addressed.** The abstract, Methods, Results and table caption state that patient retrieval conditions on 60 positive-bearing patients; ten no-positive patients are excluded from recall by definition.
9. **Task contracts — addressed.** BigMHC and PRIME are called members of the same broad immunogenicity-score category, not identical biological models.
10. **Incomplete neoantigen context — addressed.** The limitations list wild-type counterpart, expression, clonality and direct presentation evidence as absent from evaluated model inputs.

No reviewer comment was dismissed without either a code/result change or an explicit limitation.
